Why 153 sessions becomes 78

The statistic counts consecutive trading sessions below each version’s own running equity high and includes an open, unrecovered period at the end of the test. The curves can look almost identical while producing very different counts: a one-day crossing of the prior high resets the counter.

Control$63,554.20$63,272.00 · $282.20 below prior high-$2,694.602025-12-31–2026-08-11: 153 sessions; unrecovered at test end
75%$62,620.90$62,983.90 · New high by $363.00-$2,157.202025-12-31–2026-04-20: 74 below-high sessions, then recovered; 2026-04-21–2026-08-11: 78 sessions; unrecovered at test end
85%$63,413.95$63,676.75 · New high by $262.80-$2,422.702025-12-31–2026-04-20: 74 below-high sessions, then recovered; 2026-04-21–2026-08-11: 78 sessions; unrecovered at test end
Interpretation: 75% and 85% briefly reached new highs on April 20, resetting their counters, while control finished that session only $282.20 below its own high. All three versions subsequently entered another drawdown and remained below their running highs on August 11. The 153-versus-78 comparison is therefore a threshold effect, not evidence that the tightened versions spent half as much total time in drawdown.
Statistical caution: the paired bootstrap intervals are descriptive and cross zero when they do so in the table. The results show historical behavior of this fixed proxy; they do not establish that a small difference will persist out of sample.

Scope and reproducibility

Scope: fixed one-lot sizing; no compounding or portfolio resizing. The three strategies share identical entries, widths, initial stops, execution settings, and fees. Only the backtest surviving-side stop differs.
